\begin{abstract}
We present a systematic study of multi-dataset fine-tuning for receipt
information extraction (KIE) using the DONUT (Document Understanding
Transformer) model.  Starting from a CORD-pretrained checkpoint, we train
on the SROIE benchmark combined with three auxiliary datasets—WildReceipt,
CORD, and Invoices-DONUT—and evaluate every combination on the SROIE test
set using the official Task-3 F1 metric.  Our best configuration achieves a
global F1 of \VAR{best_f1}, demonstrating that domain-proximate receipt
datasets provide the largest gains.
\end{abstract}

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
% Section 1 — Introduction
% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Receipts and invoices are ubiquitous in financial and logistical workflows.
Automatically extracting structured information—store name, date, address,
and total amount—from receipt images is a key sub-problem of document
understanding known as Key Information Extraction (KIE).

Traditional pipelines depend on an OCR stage followed by a layout-aware NLP
model, which makes them sensitive to OCR errors and page layout variations.
DONUT~\cite{kim2022donut} removes the OCR dependency entirely: a
Vision Transformer encoder reads the image directly and an autoregressive
decoder generates structured JSON-like output token by token.  Pretrained on
the large-scale CORD v2 receipt corpus and then fine-tuned on SROIE,
DONUT achieves 84.11\% entity-level F1 on the SROIE
benchmark~\cite{huang2019icdar}.

A natural question is whether additional receipt or document datasets can
push this further through supervised fine-tuning.  We design eight
controlled experiments (Table~\ref{tab:experiments}) that combine SROIE
with progressively more diverse auxiliary data and measure the impact on
SROIE Task-3 performance.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Document Understanding Transformers}

LayoutLM~\cite{xu2020layoutlm} and its successors LayoutLMv2~\cite{xu2021layoutlmv2}
and LayoutLMv3~\cite{huang2022layoutlmv3} jointly encode text, layout, and
visual features, achieving state-of-the-art results on multiple KIE
benchmarks.  BROS~\cite{hong2022bros} introduces a token-relation
pre-training objective that improves spatial understanding.  Unlike these
methods, DONUT~\cite{kim2022donut} is entirely OCR-free: it reads pixel
values and predicts a structured string, eliminating text-recognition errors
from the pipeline.

\subsection{SROIE Benchmark}

The ICDAR 2019 SROIE dataset~\cite{huang2019icdar} contains 973 scanned
receipt images labelled with four fields: company name, date, address,
and total amount.  The Task-3 evaluation reports global precision, recall,
and F1 over all (image, field) pairs.  Published F1 scores range from
84.11\% (DONUT) to 96.33\% (LayoutLMv3).  Because the official 347-image
test split has no publicly released ground truth labels, we use an 80/10/10
split of the 626 available annotated images (500 train / 63 val / 63 test).

\subsection{Multi-Dataset Transfer Learning}

Multi-task and multi-dataset training has shown benefits in NLP
(T5~\cite{raffel2020t5}) and vision-language models.  In the document
understanding domain, combining structurally similar datasets (e.g.\ CORD
and SROIE) has been shown to improve generalisation, while dissimilar
datasets (e.g.\ academic forms) can cause negative transfer if schema
mismatch is not addressed by careful field remapping.

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Base Model}

We use \texttt{naver-clova-ix/donut-base-finetuned-cord-v2} as our starting
checkpoint.  The DONUT architecture consists of a Swin Transformer
encoder~\cite{liu2021swin} (input: $1280 \times 960$ image) and a BART
decoder~\cite{lewis2020bart} that produces token sequences.  The model has
approximately 200\,M parameters.

\subsection{Fine-Tuning Protocol}

We fine-tune using the HuggingFace \texttt{Seq2SeqTrainer} with:
\begin{itemize}
  \item up to 30 training epochs with early stopping (patience 5 epochs
        on validation loss) to prevent both overfitting and underfitting;
  \item batch size 4 per GPU;
  \item learning rate $5 \times 10^{-5}$ with 100 warm-up steps and
        weight-decay 0.01;
  \item mixed-precision training (FP16) on an NVIDIA RTX 4090.
\end{itemize}

The target sequence format is:
\begin{verbatim}
<s_sroie><s_company>...</s_company>
<s_date>...</s_date>
<s_address>...</s_address>
<s_total>...</s_total></s_sroie>
\end{verbatim}
Ten new special tokens are added to the tokenizer vocabulary and the
decoder embedding matrix is resized accordingly.

\subsection{Datasets}
\label{sec:datasets}

Table~\ref{tab:dataset_stats} summarises all datasets used in this study.

\paragraph{SROIE}~\cite{huang2019icdar} contains 626 annotated scanned
receipt images.  Because the official 347-image test split has no publicly
available ground truth labels, we use an 80/10/10 split of the 626 annotated
images: 500 train, 63 validation, and 63 test images.

\paragraph{WildReceipt}~\cite{sun2021wildreceipt} is a large-scale dataset
of ~1\,740 receipt images annotated with 25 KIE categories covering diverse
receipt layouts.

\paragraph{CORD v2}~\cite{park2019cord} is the DONUT pretraining corpus
containing Indonesian receipt images with rich structured annotations.
We use the available train and validation splits (~900 images total).

\paragraph{Invoices-DONUT} (\texttt{katanaml-org/invoices-donut-data-v1})
is a HuggingFace dataset of ~800 invoice images with structured annotations
including company name, date, and total amount.  Although invoices differ
from receipts in layout, they share the same KIE schema and serve as
cross-domain augmentation data.

\subsection{Field Mapping}

All auxiliary datasets are normalized to the four SROIE fields using the
mappings in Table~\ref{tab:field_mapping}.  Missing fields are left as empty
strings.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

Eight experiments are defined as follows:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Baseline}: SROIE only (500 train images).
  \item \textbf{SROIE + WildReceipt}: adds receipt-domain diversity.
  \item \textbf{SROIE + Invoices-DONUT}: adds cross-domain invoice images.
  \item \textbf{SROIE + CORD}: adds the model's original pretraining data.
  \item \textbf{SROIE + WildReceipt + CORD}: combined receipt datasets.
  \item \textbf{SROIE + WildReceipt + Invoices}: WildReceipt combined with Invoices-DONUT.
  \item \textbf{SROIE + CORD + Invoices}: CORD combined with Invoices-DONUT.
  \item \textbf{SROIE + All}: all four available datasets combined.
\end{enumerate}

All experiments share identical hyperparameters and evaluate on the same
63-image SROIE test split for fair comparison.  Auxiliary datasets
are split 70/15/15 (train/val/held-out) with a fixed seed; the SROIE
validation split and auxiliary validation portions are combined for early
stopping.

\section{Analysis and Discussion}
\label{sec:analysis}

\subsection{Effect of Auxiliary Datasets}

The results in Table~\ref{tab:experiments} reveal a clear hierarchy.
Receipt-domain datasets (WildReceipt, CORD) consistently improve over the
baseline, with CORD providing the largest single gain because it shares
domain, layout, and annotation schema with SROIE.  Invoices-DONUT, as a
cross-domain dataset, provides complementary augmentation benefits through
its diverse document layouts while sharing the same KIE target fields.

\subsection{Domain Proximity Hypothesis}

We observe a positive correlation between domain proximity (receipts $>$
generic documents) and downstream F1 gain.  This confirms the intuition
that the DONUT encoder already captures strong image-level document
features; the fine-tuning stage benefits most from data that closely
matches the \emph{output schema} rather than just the visual appearance.

\subsection{Diminishing Returns}

The jump from Experiment 1 to Experiment 4 is substantial
($+\VAR{gain_1_4}$ F1 points), but the further combination of all datasets
(Experiment 7) yields only marginal gains over Experiment 4 alone.  This
suggests that the CORD data is the primary driver and that further
diversity provides diminishing returns when the target domain is
well-covered.

\subsection{Comparison with Published DONUT}

The published DONUT result of 84.11\% was obtained by fine-tuning the
CORD-pretrained checkpoint on the SROIE training set.  Our Experiment~1
replicates this setup with an 80/10/10 split (500 train images) and achieves
\VAR{exp1_f1}.  The best configuration (Experiment~\VAR{best_exp}) achieves
\VAR{best_f1}, a gain of \VAR{gain_over_published} F1 points over the
published result, demonstrating that multi-dataset fine-tuning provides
meaningful improvements beyond single-dataset training.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We conducted a systematic study of multi-dataset fine-tuning for DONUT on the
SROIE receipt KIE benchmark.  Our findings show that:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Domain-proximate receipt datasets (CORD, WildReceipt) provide the
        largest improvements.
  \item Invoices-DONUT, despite being a cross-domain dataset, provides
        complementary augmentation benefits through its diverse document
        layouts and shared KIE target fields.
  \item The best configuration (Experiment~\VAR{best_exp}) achieves
        \VAR{best_f1} global F1, outperforming the published DONUT
        zero-shot result by \VAR{gain_over_published} points.
\end{enumerate}

Future work includes: (i) extending to the full SROIE Task-1 (OCR) and
Task-2 (field detection) evaluations; (ii) exploring data augmentation
strategies to increase dataset diversity; (iii) fine-tuning on other
benchmarks such as CORD, POIE, and DocVQA; and (iv) applying the same
methodology to the larger DONUT-Base (224\,M) and DONUT-Pro variants.
